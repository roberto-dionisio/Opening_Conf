SLIDE 3 - WHERE WE ARE, AND WHAT STOPS US                                [2:34]
===============================================================================

Let me say why we thought this particular meeting was worth organising.

Lattice field theory has become a precision science. On the left of this
picture is the regime where that is true: Euclidean, in equilibrium, at zero
chemical potential. In that corner we compute at the per-mille level. That is
a real achievement, and it took forty years.

And precisely because we are that precise, we can see very clearly where it
stops.

In the middle is the wall. It is not one obstacle. It is four, and each one
stands in front of something specific.

    Critical slowing down and topological freezing block the continuum
    limit. As the lattice spacing goes down, autocorrelation times blow up,
    and the topological charge simply stops moving.

    The sign problem blocks finite density. The measure stops being
    positive, so importance sampling stops being sampling.

    Analytic continuation blocks real-time dynamics. Going from Euclidean to
    Minkowski is an ill-posed inverse problem.

    And the exponential growth of the Hilbert space blocks anything with a
    lot of entanglement.

The first of those is not exotic and it is not in the future. It is already
here, at the lattice spacings we use today. The other three are the reason
the Hamiltonian formulation keeps coming back.

At the bottom are the three families of method being thrown at that wall:
generative models and normalising flows, tensor networks and neural quantum
states, and quantum hardware. Lattice field theory, quantum information,
machine learning, quantum many-body physics.

    -> CLICK

And here is the point. Not one of those four barriers belongs to a single one
of those communities. Not one.

That is the entire premise of this meeting. It is why we did not organise a
lattice workshop, and we did not organise a quantum simulation workshop. We
organised this.
